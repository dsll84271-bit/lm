## 1. YouTube Partner Program (YPP) requirements now and the changes from 1 February 2027

### Takeaway
From 1 February 2027 the ads/Premium tier of YPP needs 1,000 subscribers plus either 8,000 public watch hours (12 months) or 20 million Shorts views (90 days). That is double the current 4,000 hours / 10M Shorts views. Existing partners do not have to requalify. Partners must accept updated terms by 31 January 2027, a new activity standard applies, and from then on Shorts Creator Pool earnings in a given month need 10M qualified Shorts views in the past 90 days. For a 12.5k-subscriber channel not yet in YPP, the practical point is to qualify and apply under the current bar before 1 February 2027 if it can. Whether a pending application is judged under the old bar is not confirmed.

### Cited Findings
- Official YouTube Help page "Changes to the YouTube Partner Program" (answer 12843009) confirms the 1 February 2027 changes and is the source of truth — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12843009?hl=en)
- New entry thresholds from 1 Feb 2027: 1,000 subscribers plus either **8,000 qualified watch hours** or **20 million qualified Shorts views** for the ads and Premium tier. The current bar is **4,000 hours or 10 million Shorts views**. The 1,000-subscriber requirement stays. — [YouTube Help, via search extract](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12843009?hl=en); also [TechTimes, 11 Aug 2026 ("YouTube Partner Program Doubles Entry Bar, Adds Monthly Shorts Earnings Gate")](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/323954/20260811/youtube-partner-program-doubles-entry-bar-adds-monthly-shorts-earnings-gate.htm); [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-partner-program-changes-2027/)
- The announcement appears to date from about August 2026, going by TechTimes' 11 Aug 2026 dateline — [TechTimes](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/323954/20260811/youtube-partner-program-doubles-entry-bar-adds-monthly-shorts-earnings-gate.htm)
- Grandfathering: "Current partners are not required to requalify"; creators already in YPP keep their status — [YouTube Help, via search extract](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12843009?hl=en)
- New activity standard. A channel counts as active if it meets any **one** of: 1,000 qualified watch hours in the past 365 days; 1 million qualified Shorts views in the past 90 days; or 2 long-form videos **or** 5 Shorts uploaded every 90 days. Channels that fall below get an extended 90-day window to recover. — [YouTube Help, via search extract](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12843009?hl=en)
- Shorts earnings gate: a channel needs **10 million qualified Shorts views over the past 90 days** to earn from the Shorts Creator Pool that month. Search Engine Journal cites YouTube's help pages and reports it as an addition to the YPP terms. — [Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-updates-partner-program-terms-shorts-payout-rules/585331/). Third-party guides describe this gate inconsistently; the official page should take precedence.
- Terms deadline: creators must review and accept the updated terms in YouTube Studio by **31 January 2027**. Those who don't will stop earning from the associated monetisation features from 1 February 2027. — [YouTube Help, via search extract](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12843009?hl=en)
- No change to eligibility for fan funding, YouTube Creator Partnerships or YouTube Shopping — [YouTube Help, via search extract](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12843009?hl=en)
- Secondary explainers covering the same change: [Lilach Bullock](https://www.lilachbullock.com/youtube-partner-program-changes-2027/), [TubeGen](https://www.tubegen.ai/blog/youtube-partner-program-changes-2027), [TechJournal](https://techjournal.org/youtube-partner-program-2027), [AIR Media-Tech 2026 guide](https://air.io/en/monetization/youtube-partner-program-requirements-2026-the-complete-guide)

### Inferences
- A 12.5k-subscriber channel already clears the subscriber bar. The binding constraint is watch hours: 4,000 now, 8,000 after 1 Feb 2027, counted over the trailing 12 months.
- Hours from videos that are made private or deleted stop counting toward the public watch-hour total [training knowledge, unverified this session: YouTube counts only "valid public watch hours"]. Cleaning up old compilations can therefore cut eligible hours. The creator should check the YPP progress tracker in Studio before and after any clean-up.
- Shorts views do not count toward the watch-hours path; that path counts long-form public watch hours [training knowledge, unverified this session]. For a documentary channel, the long-form route (8,000 hours) is far more realistic than 20M Shorts views.
- The lower "expanded" or fan-funding tier is unchanged by the 2027 rules. Its thresholds as of 2023–2025 were 500 subscribers, 3 public uploads in 90 days, and either 3,000 watch hours (12 months) or 3M Shorts views (90 days). [Training knowledge, unverified this session; thresholds may differ by country, so check for the UK.] That tier gives memberships, Super Thanks and similar, but no ad revenue.
- Revenue shares, for modelling: long-form ads 55% to the creator; Shorts 45% of the creator's allocated share of the Shorts pool [training knowledge from the 2023 YPP terms, unverified this session].

### Gaps
- Could not confirm whether a channel that **applies** before 1 Feb 2027 but is still **under review** on that date is judged under the old 4,000-hour bar. The help page could not be fetched. This is the most decision-relevant open question for the creator.
- Could not verify the exact current UK thresholds for the expanded/fan-funding tier.
- Could not read whether the new activity standard can remove YPP status from existing partners after the 90-day grace window, or only pause features.

## 2. Reused content policy and the inauthentic content policy (July 2025, clarified again in July 2026)

### Takeaway
There are two separate channel-level monetisation policies. **Reused content** covers material taken from others without significant original commentary, modification or value. It is the policy that rejects compilation, clip-edit and football-comp channels, and YouTube says it was not changed in 2025. **Inauthentic content**, formerly "repetitious content", was renamed on 15 July 2025 to cover "mass-produced or repetitive" content. It targets templated output, not AI as such, and blogs report a further restructuring around 16 July 2026. AI narration is allowed if the videos carry real original value and are not near-identical templates. A channel applying with old third-party compilations still public is at high risk of a reused content rejection, because reviewers judge the channel as a whole.

### Cited Findings
**July 2025 change (inauthentic content)**
- The "repetitious content" rule was renamed "inauthentic content" to make clear it covers mass-produced or repetitive content. Effective **15 July 2025**. — [TechCrunch, 9 Jul 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/07/09/youtube-prepares-crackdown-on-mass-produced-and-repetitive-videos-as-concern-over-ai-slop-grows); [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/technology/youtube-updates-monetisation-policies-ai-and-repetitive-content-ban-begins-july-15-1.500192660)
- YouTube described it as a clarification of existing guidelines, not a new policy. Mass-produced or repetitive content was already ineligible for monetisation. — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/youtube-clarifies-monetization-update-inauthentic-repeated-content/752892/); [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/youtube-clarifies-inauthentic-content-policy-changes/)
- YouTube said its reused content policies, which cover commentary, compilations and reaction videos, remain **unchanged**. AI is still welcome if creators follow the rules and add original value such as commentary, editing or narration. — [eWeek](https://www.eweek.com/news/youtube-responds-to-ai-concerns/); [Plagiarism Today](https://www.plagiarismtoday.com/2025/07/08/youtube-targets-inauthentic-content/)
- The inauthentic content policy applies to **entire channels**, not individual videos — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/youtube-clarifies-monetization-update-inauthentic-repeated-content/752892/)
- Policy examples of mass-produced content, as quoted by secondary sources: channels uploading "narrative stories with only superficial differences between them" and "slideshows that all have the same narration" — [Exemplary.ai](https://exemplary.ai/blog/youtube-inauthentic-content-policy); [GoFaceless](https://www.gofaceless.ai/en/blog/what-qualifies-as-inauthentic-content-on-youtube). Other examples cited: templated list videos with the same narration, transitions and conclusions where only the list items change; songs only slightly altered in pitch or speed; compilations without original commentary — [Overseeros audit of 3,096 videos](https://www.overseeros.com/blog/youtube-inauthentic-content-study); [TubeBuddy](https://www.tubebuddy.com/blog/youtube-ai-monetization-best-practices/)

**July 2026 restructuring (secondary sources only, details conflict)**
- Several blogs report clarified YPP guidance published **16 July 2026**, with the monetisation policies page rebuilt into sections and no changelog entry — [OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/blog/youtube-updates-july-2026); [CreatorBlade](https://creatorblade.com/blog/youtube-inauthentic-content-policy-2026-stay-monetized); [Deeporax](https://deeporax.com/blog/youtube-inauthentic-content-policy-2026)
- Reported three categories of non-monetisable content: (1) generic or repetitive/templated content; (2) unsatisfying or off-putting content; (3) AI personas giving advice on sensitive topics such as health, finance or legal matters, or presenting as human experts. Sources word the third category differently. — [NewMoneyMatrix](https://newmoneymatrix.org/youtube-inauthentic-content-policy-explained/); [Versely](https://www.versely.studio/blog/inauthentic-content-is-the-policy-that-decides-whether-ai-video-earns); [ChannelTerminated](https://channelterminated.com/demonetized/inauthentic-content/)
- One source says the heading was renamed again to "Generic or Repetitive Content"; others still call it "inauthentic content". **Unconfirmed.** — [OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/blog/youtube-updates-july-2026) vs [Faceless.my](https://faceless.my/youtube/youtube-inauthentic-content-policy-faceless-ai-2026/)
- Reported YouTube position: channels dedicated to this content are removed from YPP "regardless of whether the content is AI-generated or not". Clips, compilations and reactions remain under the unchanged reused content policy. — [secondary, via search summary of CreatorBlade/ArWriterAI](https://arwriterai.com/en/blog/youtube-inauthentic-content-policy-ai-creators-2026/)

**Reused content (the policy most relevant to old compilations, edits and football comps)**
- Core test: reused content is material from YouTube or elsewhere reuploaded without enough original value. The question is whether viewers can see a meaningful difference between the original and your video. The policy applies **even with permission** from the original creator. — [vidIQ guide](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-reused-content-policy-guide/); [Quasa](https://quasa.io/media/youtube-s-reused-content-policy-what-counts-as-transformation-and-why-permission-is-not-s)
- Generally allowed: critical reviews (for example of a movie scene) with commentary; reactions with genuine commentary; edited footage with voiceover; compilations whose editing tells a story — [Thinkific](https://www.thinkific.com/blog/youtube-reused-content/); [TunePocket](https://www.tunepocket.com/reused-content-youtube-guide/)
- Generally not allowed: clips of a TV show or movie edited into a compilation without modification or commentary; reaction videos with no significant added value; collections of songs from different artists; promotion of others' content — [Lilach Bullock](https://www.lilachbullock.com/youtube-reused-content-policy/); [TunePocket](https://www.tunepocket.com/reused-content-youtube-guide/)
- There is no fixed percentage of original commentary that qualifies. "Editing density is not equivalent to editorial value." — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-reused-content-policy-guide/)
- Official wording [training knowledge, unverified this session; YouTube Help answer 1311392]: the policy requires channels that reuse others' content to add "significant original commentary, substantive modifications, or educational or entertainment value". Listed examples of disallowed content include "compilations of short videos from other social media websites" and "clips from other creators' content… edited together with little or no narrative". — [YouTube channel monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en) (could not be fetched; re-verify the wording)

**What to do with old compilation/edit videos before applying**
- Reviewers reportedly check the channel's main theme, most-viewed videos, newest videos, watch-time leaders, titles, thumbnails, descriptions and About section — [AIR Media-Tech](https://air.io/en/youtube-hacks/reused-content-monetization-rejected)
- Recommended clean-up: make private or delete videos that consist mainly of third-party content, add real commentary to the rest, and upload new original videos before (re)applying — [AIR Media-Tech](https://air.io/en/youtube-hacks/reused-content-monetization-rejected). YouTube's rejection FAQ, as quoted by secondary sources, says to "edit or delete the videos that break the policies" before reapplying — [Awarexone](https://www.awarexone.com/research/youtube-monetisation-reused-content)
- After a rejection: appeal within 21 days, or reapply after 30 days for a first rejection and 90 days for later rejections or a rejected appeal — [ChannelTerminated](https://channelterminated.com/demonetized/reused-content/); [Awarexone](https://www.awarexone.com/research/youtube-monetisation-reused-content). An appeal on an unchanged channel rarely helps; if the format has to change, plan to reapply. — [AIR Media-Tech](https://air.io/en/youtube-hacks/reused-content-monetization-rejected)
- Beware paid "remove the reused content flag" or "guaranteed YPP" offers; they are scams — [Awarexone](https://www.awarexone.com/research/terminated-youtube-partner-channel-monetisation-appeals)

### Inferences
- For a 12.5k-subscriber channel with old compilations, football comps and clip edits, these are almost certainly reused content and are likely to trigger rejection if they are prominent: high views, recent, or watch-time leaders. Two options:
  - **Private** keeps the videos and their comments recoverable. Reviewers should not see them, but their watch hours stop counting toward the public-hours threshold.
  - **Delete** is permanent and also removes any copyright-claim history tied to those videos. There is no evidence that deleting helps more than making private for the YPP review itself.

  A reasonable sequence: build up enough new original documentary hours, check the Studio eligibility tracker, make the compilations private (or unlisted is not enough: assume reviewers can still find them; treat this as unverified), then apply. Do this before 1 Feb 2027 if the 4,000-hour bar can be reached in time.
- Football compilations are a particular risk. They draw Content ID from rights holders (Premier League, broadcasters), and they would make the channel's "main theme" read as sports clips rather than documentaries.
- For the new format, the main inauthentic-content risk is sameness: identical intro, outro, music bed, narration cadence, stock-footage pattern and title template across many uploads. Vary structure, write original scripts with sourced facts, and add original visuals (motion graphics, maps, diagrams).
- The July 2026 "AI personas on sensitive topics" category does not appear to cover film/game history and science explainers. Avoid presenting the AI narrator as a human expert, especially on health topics (for example "the real medicine behind House").

### Gaps
- Could not retrieve verbatim current (post-July 2026) text of YouTube's monetisation policy page. The three-category structure and any renaming are reported only by blogs, and they conflict.
- No official statement found on whether reviewers see unlisted videos, or on private versus delete in YPP review.
- No official data on how many hours of new original content a channel needs before reviewers judge its "main theme" as changed.

## 3. Disclosure of AI-generated or synthetic content (altered or synthetic content label)

### Takeaway
YouTube requires disclosure, via the "AI use" / altered-or-synthetic toggle in Studio, only for **realistic** content that viewers could mistake for real people, places or events. A generic AI narrator voice reading an original script over real archival footage or clearly illustrative graphics does not, per most guides, need the label. Cloning a real person's voice, realistic AI-generated "historical" footage, or realistic altered footage of real events does. Disclosure does not affect monetisation eligibility; repeatedly failing to disclose can lead to penalties including YPP suspension.

### Cited Findings
- Official help page: "Disclosing use of altered or synthetic content" (answer 14328491). In the Attributes section under "AI use", creators select Yes if the content meets the disclosure requirements. The test is realism: realistic AI content and meaningful alterations require disclosure; clearly unrealistic content or minor edits do not. — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491?hl=en)
- A generic AI voiceover over truthful footage, or over stock or illustrative visuals, carries no disclosure obligation on its own. The trigger is a voice imitating a real person, or making a real person appear to say or do something they did not. — [Vois.so](https://vois.so/blog/youtube-ai-voice-disclosure-rules); [AIR Media-Tech](https://air.io/en/youtube-hacks/youtube-ai-disclosure-in-2026-label-it-yourself-or-youtube-will); [Versely](https://www.versely.studio/blog/youtube-ai-disclosure-exemptions-list)
- Conflict on voice cloning: one guide says cloning **your own** voice is exempt. Another lists AI dubs that recreate a creator's natural voice, and cloned voices of real people, as needing the label. — [Minimatters](https://minimatters.com/youtube-altered-or-synthetic-content-disclosure/) vs [Hooklab](https://hooklab.co.uk/blog/youtube-disclosure-rules-for-ai-generated-likeness-and-synthetic-media)
- Realistic AI-generated music is listed among items needing disclosure — [ShortsFast](https://shortsfast.com/blog/youtube-ai-content-disclosure-rules-2026/)
- Disclosing does not limit monetisation eligibility; consistently failing to disclose when required can lead to penalties, including suspension from YPP — [NoFaceTube](https://nofacetube.com/rules/youtube-altered-synthetic-content-disclosure/); [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491?hl=en)
- A 2026 guide's framing: "label it yourself or YouTube will". YouTube may apply labels itself, for example for C2PA-tagged content or content generated with its own AI tools. — [AIR Media-Tech](https://air.io/en/youtube-hacks/youtube-ai-disclosure-in-2026-label-it-yourself-or-youtube-will)

### Inferences
- For this channel: a stock or synthetic narrator voice (not imitating anyone) needs no label. AI-generated realistic reconstructions, for example a photoreal "1960s NASA control room" or a fake "archival" photo of a real event, should be labelled. Stylised or obviously illustrative AI art probably does not need one, but labelling borderline items is cheap insurance because it has no monetisation cost.
- Separately from YouTube rules, saying in the description that narration is AI-generated helps credibility with viewers and reviewers. It is not legally required in the UK as far as found.

### Gaps
- Could not read the live official example list (the "requires disclosure" / "doesn't require disclosure" tables) to confirm the voice-cloning wording verbatim.
- No evidence found either way on whether self-disclosed AI narration affects recommendation reach.

## 4. RPM/CPM ranges (2025–2026) by niche and geography; long-form vs Shorts; TV viewing

### Takeaway
Third-party data puts median long-form RPM at roughly $2–3 overall. Entertainment (about $2.40) and gaming (about $2.05) sit near the bottom, and education/science much higher (about $10 median in one 300-channel study). A history/science-behind-fiction channel will likely land between entertainment and education depending on how YouTube and advertisers classify it, plausibly about $3–6 RPM with a strong US/UK audience. Shorts pay roughly $0.02–0.08 per 1,000 views, about 1/50th to 1/100th of long-form. UK RPM is estimated at about 85–95% of US. YouTube now has a record 15.8% of US TV-screen time, but no reliable public data quantifies a TV-viewing RPM premium.

### Cited Findings
- AIR Media-Tech study of 300 channels (May 2025–May 2026), median RPM: **Education & Science $10.22; Entertainment $2.43; Gaming $2.05**; overall median about **$2.30**; Kids & Teens $0.33 — [Mubert / AIR data](https://mubert.com/blog/youtube-rpm-by-niche-and-country); [Overseeros](https://www.overseeros.com/blog/youtube-rpm-by-niche)
- Other 2026 estimates vary widely. Online Education $5–7.50 and Gaming (reviews) $4–6 for US audiences in one; Education $8–18 and Gaming $2–5 in another — [YTCalculators](https://ytcalculators.com/blog/youtube-rpm-by-niche/); [Fluxnote](https://fluxnote.io/blog/youtube-rpm-by-niche-2026); [ClipFlux medians](https://clipflux.ai/guides/youtube-rpm-by-niche)

UK estimated at **0.85–0.95× US** — [YTMoneyCalculator](https://ytmoneycalculator.com/blog/average-youtube-rpm/); [LearningRevolution](https://www.learningrevolution.net/youtube-earnings-calculator/)
- Seasonality: median RPM peaked at $2.35 in November and fell to $1.56 in January. Entertainment had the smallest Q4 premium (+4%). AIR is an outlier at UK $0.166. — [CreatorBlade](https://creatorblade.com/blog/youtube-shorts-monetization-2026); [Fluxnote](https://fluxnote.io/guides/youtube-shorts-vs-long-form-rpm); [AIR Media-Tech](https://air.io/en/monetization/what-rpm-can-you-expect-from-shorts-in-2026)
- Long-form: "$3–5 is average, $8–20+ is strong" (tech/finance/business); overall range $0.03–$25 depending on niche, average $2.50–5 — [YTMoneyCalculator](https://ytmoneycalculator.com/blog/how-much-youtube-pay-per-1000-views/); [AIVideoToolbox](https://aivideotoolbox.com/youtube-rpm-calculator)
- TV viewing: YouTube reached **15.8% of all US TV time in August 2026** (Nielsen The Gauge), a record for any platform and more than double Netflix's 7.7%. It was 14.2% in July 2026 and 13.8% in June 2026. — [Tubefilter, 22 Oct 2026](https://www.tubefilter.com/2026/10/22/nielsen-the-gauge-youtube-record-watch-time-share-us-tv/); [Pasquale Pillitteri](https://pasqualepillitteri.it/en/news/33600/youtube-hits-record-15-8-of-us-tv-time)
- CTV reportedly accounts for about 36% of YouTube watch time, with longer sessions than mobile. The methodology is unverified. — [Vidpuff](https://vidpuff.com/blog/youtube-watch-time-statistics)

### Inferences
- Rough model for this channel (assumption-heavy): at $3–6 RPM, 100k long-form views a month gives about $300–600 a month; 1M Shorts views a month at about $0.05 gives about $50. Long-form documentaries of 12–25 minutes allow mid-roll ads (8+ minutes) [training knowledge], which is where most of the money is.
- "History/science behind fiction" sits between the entertainment and education categories. Framing that skews educational (science, engineering, real history) probably attracts better advertisers than pure film/game commentary, but no data isolates this sub-niche.
- TV viewing favours long, documentary-style videos, the format this channel produces. Advertisers generally pay more for CTV inventory, but I found no creator-side data quantifying the RPM uplift. Treat any claimed "TV premium" as unverified.
- November–December is the best time to launch strong uploads for RPM. January RPM is about a third lower.

### Gaps

- No official or credible data on RPM by device (TV vs mobile).
- All RPM figures are third-party estimates with varied methods; YouTube publishes none.

## 5. YouTube "Shows", series and podcast features (2025–2026)

### Takeaway
From about July 2026, YPP creators can convert playlists into **Shows**, an episodic format built for TV-screen viewing with sequential playback and placement in "Continue watching" / "Recommended shows". Podcasts are a separate feature, in which a podcast is a playlist and each episode a video. No official data shows that Shows boost watch time or recommendations. The format suits a documentary channel with recurring series, but it is only available after YPP entry.

### Cited Findings
- Creators in YPP can convert existing playlists into Shows, a format for episodic content viewed on TV screens, so viewers can watch episodes in sequence in a streaming-service style. Reported July 2026. — [Creator Handbook](https://www.creatorhandbook.net/youtube-lets-creators-turn-playlists-into-shows/)
- Shows reportedly get placement in "Continue watching" and "Recommended shows" — [Backbeat Recording Studio](https://www.biggerbetterbackbeat.com/youtube-shows-vs-podcasts/)
- On YouTube, a podcast is a playlist and each episode a video. Shows are a separate feature. Some playlists are not eligible as podcasts, including content not owned by the creator. — [Lilach Bullock](https://www.lilachbullock.com/youtube-podcasts/)
- YouTube Music is testing an AI-voiced weekly podcast guide for Premium subscribers (Sept 2026) — [Android Headlines](https://www.androidheadlines.com/2026/09/youtube-musics-new-podcast-feature-talks-you-into-your-next-favorite-show.html)

### Inferences
- Plan content as named series from the start (for example "The Real Science of…", "History Behind the Game") in ordered playlists. They can be converted to Shows on YPP entry, and they help sequential binge sessions on TV.
- Any benefit to recommendations is plausible, because session watch time is a known ranking input, but it is **unproven**: there is no official statement or data.

### Gaps
- Could not access YouTube's official Shows help page or announcement, or check eligibility criteria beyond YPP membership.
- No evidence (official or creator case study) on watch-time or recommendation uplift from Shows.

## 6. Copyright: fair use for commentary with film stills, posters, trailer clips; Content ID; claims vs strikes; safe visual sources

### Takeaway
Fair use (US) or fair dealing (UK) is a legal defence argued after a dispute. It does not stop Content ID matching. Short trailer clips with commentary are often tracked or monetised by studios rather than blocked, but that tolerance is the studio's to withdraw. A Content ID claim typically diverts or shares revenue and does not hurt channel standing; a copyright strike (a formal takedown) does, and three strikes in 90 days means termination. The safest visuals are your own motion graphics, US federal government works (NASA, with caveats), public-domain archives, CC0, and correctly attributed CC-licensed Wikimedia files.

### Cited Findings
- A Content ID claim is an automated fingerprint match that does not count against the channel and mainly affects monetisation (revenue goes to, or is shared with, the claimant). A strike is a formal legal takedown; **three strikes in 90 days means channel termination**. — [Wikipedia: YouTube copyright strike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YouTube_copyright_strike); [Hashtag Network](https://www.hashtagnetwork.net/copyright/copyright-claim-vs-strike); [TubeAnalytics](https://www.tubeanalytics.net/blog/youtube-copyright-claims-strikes-complete-guide)
- "Fair use is a defence you argue after a dispute — it is not a shield that stops Content ID from matching your video." "Content ID cannot evaluate fair use — it only matches fingerprints." — [eCopyright.io](https://www.ecopyright.io/blog/youtube-creator-copyright-strikes-guide/); [YTCalculators](https://ytcalculators.com/blog/youtube-copyright-guide/)
- Trailers: trailer commentary is "heavily tolerated (it markets the film)", and studio Content ID policies on trailers often track or monetise rather than block, but the tolerance is the studio's to withdraw. Official promotional material (trailers, press kits) is still studio copyright. — [Downloader.website guide](https://downloader.website/how-to-download-and-use-movie-trailers-clips-and-press-kits-); [Trademarkia](https://www.trademarkia.com/news/entertainment/movie-clips-youtube-copyright)
- Keep clips short and visibly transformed with commentary. Compiling many clips does not make the use transformative, because each clip is matched individually. — [Outside IP Counsel](https://outsideipcounsel.com/guides/using-movie-tv-clips-in-videos/)
- Improved recognition can identify short clips and stills even after edits, which raises claim rates — [YTCalculators](https://ytcalculators.com/blog/youtube-copyright-guide/)
- Fair-use case note: a reaction video lacking transformative commentary, with potential market harm, was held not to be fair use — [JD Supra](https://www.jdsupra.com/legalnews/reaction-videos-and-copyright-law-best-9687112/); see also [Fasken](https://www.fasken.com/en/knowledge/2021/06/everyones-a-critic-copyright-considerations-for-youtube-and-twitch-reaction-videos-part-ii) (2021, possibly outdated)
- NASA: NASA-produced imagery is generally usable for educational and informational purposes without explicit permission under its Media Usage Guidelines. The NASA insignia, logotype and identifiers are protected and must not imply endorsement. Identifiable people may have publicity rights. Third-party content on NASA sites is not automatically public domain; for example the Event Horizon Telescope black hole image is CC BY 4.0 and requires attribution. — [NASA Images & Media guidelines](https://nasa.gov/nasa-brand-center/images-and-media); [NASA Film & Documentary Guidelines](https://www.nasa.gov/nasa-brand-center/film-and-documentary-guidelines/); [Plagiarism Today, Nov 2024 (pre-2025, possibly outdated)](https://www.plagiarismtoday.com/2024/11/04/nasa-copyright-and-trademark-in-space/)

### Inferences
- The creator is in the UK. UK law has no general "fair use". Its fair dealing exceptions include "criticism, review and quotation" (CDPA 1988 s.30) [training knowledge, unverified this session]. In practice YouTube disputes run through US DMCA processes, but a UK creator's legal exposure is to UK law as well. A video essay that analyses a film's science with brief, attributed clips fits the criticism/review/quotation logic better than b-roll filler does.
- Lowest-risk visual hierarchy for this channel:
  1. Own motion graphics, diagrams, maps and text animation.
  2. US federal works: NASA (see the logo and person caveats), NOAA, USGS, and Library of Congress items marked "no known restrictions" [training knowledge; verify the rights statement per item].
  3. Public-domain archives (Internet Archive pre-1929 film, Prelinger, and items whose PD status is clear) [training knowledge].
  4. CC0 and Wikimedia Commons files with per-file licence checks. CC BY needs on-screen or description attribution; CC BY-SA needs attribution and, arguably, share-alike [training knowledge, unverified this session].
  5. Licensed stock.

  Film stills, posters and short trailer clips are higher risk. Use them sparingly, briefly and with on-screen commentary, and expect occasional claims rather than strikes.
- Avoid using full-length or long continuous trailer segments, game cutscene rips or anime episode clips as background b-roll. That is where both Content ID claims and reused content judgments concentrate.
- AI-generated "lookalike" images of copyrighted characters carry separate trademark/copyright risk and may need disclosure if they look realistic.
