## Product Overview
**One-liner:** Hypothetical You: "What if...?" stories where *you* are the hero, told with real science.
**What it does:** Each 8+ minute episode drops the viewer (a faceless orange-headed hero in a purple hoodie) into an impossible situation, plays out the fun wish-fulfilment version, then shows the real science and a small life lesson, ending on a twist.
**Product category:** YouTube channel, animated science / "what if" explainers.
**Product type:** Free long-form video (3D cel-shaded animation, narrated).
**Business model:** YouTube ad revenue and subscribers *(confirm: sponsors or merch later?)*.

## Target Audience
**Target viewers:** Curious kids and young teens, about 8–14 *(confirm)*, plus parents and teachers who watch with them.
**Primary use case:** Entertaining, re-watchable videos that leave the viewer knowing something real.
**Jobs to be done:**
- "Let me imagine having a superpower / living an impossible day."
- "Teach me something true without it feeling like school."
**Use cases:**
- After-school watching, binge sessions, classroom "fun Friday" videos.

## Problems & Pain Points
**Core problem:** Most "what if" videos are either pure fantasy with no real science, or dry explainers with no story.
**Why alternatives fall short:**
- Fact channels list facts with no character to care about.
- Fantasy/animation channels skip the science or get it wrong.
**Emotional tension:** Wanting to be special / powerful, vs. the real lesson that being yourself and being seen matters.

## Competitive Landscape
**Direct:** Other "what if" animated channels (e.g. What If, Life Noggin, Kurzgesagt for older viewers): often third-person, less personal.
**Secondary:** Fact/list channels: facts without story.
**Indirect:** Gaming and short-form video competing for the same screen time.

## Differentiation
**Key differentiators:**
- Second person: "you" are the hero in every episode.
- Every science claim is real and sourced (each episode's `script.md` lists sources).
- A full story arc with an emotional turn and a twist ending (e.g. the dog in Episode 3).
- Consistent faceless hero and 3D cel-shaded look.
**Why viewers choose us:** It's a story first, so they stay; the facts stick because they're part of the story.

## Objections
| Objection | Response |
|-----------|----------|
| "Is this just made up?" | Every fact is real and named on screen (years, universities, numbers). |
| "Too childish for me." | Science and stakes are real; the story lands for adults too. |

**Anti-persona:** Viewers who want deep technical lectures or pure gaming content.

## Customer Language
**Words to use:** what if, imagine, you, real science, actually, wait until the end
**Words to avoid:** jargon without explanation; clickbait claims the video doesn't pay off

## Brand Voice
| Attribute | Means | Doesn't mean | Example line |
|-----------|-------|--------------|--------------|
| Playful | Funny, specific moments | Random or silly for its own sake | "That's a long time to be a floating breakfast." |
| Honest | Real facts, real limits | Lecturing | "The catch? It worked for microwaves, not light." |
| Warm | Ends on something kind | Preachy | "Being seen is scary. But it's the only way anyone can clap for you." |

**Tone by context:**
- Social: short, curious hooks ("Would you still be good if no one could see you?")
- Titles: "What If You Were ___?"
- Descriptions: one-line hook, the story setup, the real science list, chapters, a question for comments.
**Never use:** fake facts, invented numbers, "you won't believe"-style bait.
