## This case's world (what changes per episode)

- **Accent:** Miami lab teal `#1FA4A0`; secondary courtroom amber `#D9A441`. Red only on the house stamps, so nothing reads as blood.
- **Paper:** legal pleading paper (numbered margin) for rulings, quotes and transcripts.
- **Signature prop:** clean microscope slides; the slide box with ten slots is the Reality Score meter.
- **Motif arrangement:** vibraphone, synth bass and brushes; muted trumpet for the courtroom; a clock under Exhibit D (`whatif/tools/case003_music.py`).

## Care points (advertiser-friendly and fair)

- No blood imagery, no kill-room footage, no crimes described. Trailer clips are short, silent, and show only offices, streets, a hospital bed and Dexter talking.
- No child-victim cases (Camm and Rea left out). Golden State Killer, Ridgway and Keyes appear only as *how they were caught*.
- Joe Bryan: say "paroled", never "pardoned" or "exonerated"; his conviction stands (Texas CCA denied habeas, 15 Jan 2020).
- Texas did **not** require accreditation for blood spatter analysis in the end (exempted Dec 2018; voluntary licence Aug 2019), so the script doesn't claim it.
- Farak: "24,075 drug charges in 16,449 cases" (charges, not convictions).
- No implication of wrongdoing at Miami-Dade's real lab; the script says so.
- "Linkage blindness" attributed to criminologists (Steven Egger, 1984), not the FBI.
- The Gary, Indiana algorithm: written by journalist Thomas Hargrove (Murder Accountability Project came later).
- Flock's "20 billion scans a month" is labelled as the company's own figure.
- The survival estimate ("months, a few years at most") is presented as the Detective's judgement, not a statistic.
