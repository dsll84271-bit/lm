# Case File 002: The Real Ebenezer Scrooge Was Even Worse

**Title (agreed):** "The Real Ebenezer Scrooge Was Even Worse"
**Hook for the moment:** *Ebenezer* (Paramount, 2026), dir. Ti West, Johnny Depp as Scrooge (with Rupert Grint, Ian McKellen, Daisy Ridley, Sam Claflin), opened No. 1 in the US on 13–15 Nov 2026 ($19M domestic, $42M worldwide opening).
**Angle:** Scrooge wasn't one man. Dickens never named a model, but the real misers he knew about were worse than Scrooge, the name may be a hoax, and the cruelty Scrooge stands for was real law.
**Reality Score:** 7/10.

## Sources (checked 25–26 Nov 2026)

- **John Elwes** (1714–1789), MP for Berkshire 1772–1784. Primary source: Edward Topham, *The Life of the Late John Elwes, Esquire* (1790; 12th ed. read on archive.org, item `lifeoflatejohnel05toph`). Topham knew him personally. Quotes used:
  - "dined upon the remaining part of a moor-hen, which had been brought out of the river by a rat!" and "an undigested part of a pike, which a larger one had swallowed"
  - "he would sit in wet clothes, sooner than have a fire to dry them"; "eat his provisions in the last stage of putrefaction"
  - "he wore a wig for above a fortnight, which I saw him pick up out of a rut in a lane … the cast-off wig of some beggar"
  - He and his uncle Sir Harvey went to bed at nightfall: "going to bed saved candle-light"
  - "never pay a toll if you can avoid it"
  - furniture: "a couple of beds, a couple of chairs, a table, and an old woman"
  - the old woman found "stretched out lifeless on a rug upon the floor … dead about two days", while Elwes himself lay near death
  - "worth nearly eight hundred thousand pounds"
  - Secondary: Wikipedia, John Elwes (politician); Merryweather, *Lives and Anecdotes of Misers* (1850), ch. IX (exclassics.com/misers/misers010.htm)
- **Dickens knew these misers:** Elwes is mentioned in *Bleak House* (1853) and *Our Mutual Friend* (1865); in OMF Book 3 ch. 5, Silas Wegg reads Merryweather's *Lives and Anecdotes of Misers* aloud, listing "John Elwes … Daniel Dancer", including Dancer's hoard in a dunghill ("a sum but little short of two thousand five hundred pounds"). Text: exclassics.com/misers/dickens.htm. These books are after 1843, so they prove Dickens knew the stories, not that Scrooge was based on them. Script says Dickens never named his model.
- **Jemmy Wood** (1756–1836), Gloucester Old Bank, "The Gloucester Miser", about £900,000, "the richest commoner in His Majesty's dominions"; coffin reportedly stoned at his funeral; will dispute, executor John Chadborn hanged himself in 1839. Source: Wikipedia, Jemmy Wood (citing Savory, *Life and Anecdotes of Jemmy Wood*, 1883). Scrooge link = local tradition (a plaque), not proven.
- **Daniel Dancer** (1716–1794): clothes of hay bands, one meal a day. Wikipedia, Daniel Dancer; Merryweather via OMF.
- **Scroggie gravestone:** Ebenezer Lennox Scroggie, "meal man" misread as "mean man"; called "a probable Dickens hoax", no corroborating evidence; stone lost in 1932. Wikipedia, A Christmas Carol / Ebenezer Scrooge; Atlas Obscura, Canongate Kirkyard.
- **The 1843 report:** Second Report of the Children's Employment Commission (Trades and Manufactures), February 1843. Found some children started work at three or four, often at five, most in regular work by seven or eight (contemporary summaries, e.g. Trove 7 Sep 1843 reprint). Dickens planned a pamphlet, "An Appeal to the People of England, on behalf of the Poor Man's Child"; letter to Dr Southwood Smith (March 1843): "a Sledge hammer has come down with twenty times the force—twenty thousand times the force—I could exert by following out my first idea". Cornish tin mines, Field Lane Ragged School; Manchester Athenaeum speech 5 Oct 1843; written in six weeks; published 19 Dec 1843. Source: Wikipedia, A Christmas Carol (Slater, Douglas-Fairhurst, Kelly).
- **"Are there no prisons? … the Union workhouses?"**: the New Poor Law 1834 workhouses; echo of Carlyle's *Chartism* (1840); "decrease the surplus population" reflects Malthus. Source: Wikipedia, A Christmas Carol (Elwell).
- **Ignorance and Want:** inspired by children Dickens saw at a ragged school. Text of *A Christmas Carol* (public domain).
- **Dickens at 12:** father John in the Marshalsea debtors' prison (1824); Charles worked ten-hour days at Warren's Blacking Warehouse, Hungerford Stairs, for six shillings a week; "how I could have been so easily cast away at such an age". Source: Wikipedia, Charles Dickens (Forster, *Life of Charles Dickens*).
- **Tiny Tim:** Dickens's disabled nephew Henry. Marley's chains: prisoners Dickens saw in Pittsburgh's Western Penitentiary, 1842. Source: Wikipedia, A Christmas Carol.

## Care points
- Dickens never said who Scrooge was based on: say "historians point to", "the men Dickens knew about".
- The wig came from "a rut in a lane" (Topham), not a hedge.
- The housekeeper: say she was found dead about two days, while Elwes himself was found near death. Don't say he killed her.
- Scroggie = probable hoax.
