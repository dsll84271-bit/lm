# Episode 2: What If You Could Read Minds?

One continuous story about YOU, present tense, escalating, a real number in every scene, a cost, a rescue, a twist, then a question for the comments. One narrator (Kokoro "Heart"). Thoughts and speech appear on screen; nobody else speaks out loud. About 9 to 10 minutes.

Facts used, and where they come from:
- The human brain has about 86 billion neurons (Azevedo and colleagues, 2009). It runs on roughly 20 watts, about a fifth of the body's energy at rest.
- Neurons talk with tiny electrical pulses; a thought is a pattern of activity across many of them.
- In 2023, researchers at the University of Texas at Austin (Tang, Huth and colleagues) built a "semantic decoder" that turned fMRI brain scans into the rough gist of stories a person heard or imagined. Each person spent about 16 hours in the scanner to train it, and it only worked on people who cooperated.
- EEG reads electrical activity through the scalp; it can't read thoughts word for word.
- A 2020 study from Queen's University estimated people have about 6,200 separate "thoughts" a day (by tracking changes in brain activity patterns). It's an estimate.
- Polygraph "lie detectors" measure heart rate, breathing and sweat: signs of stress, not lies themselves. Scientists widely dispute their accuracy.
- People speak at roughly 150 words a minute.
- The brain is about 2% of body weight but uses about 20% of its energy at rest.
- A 2021 study found the average dog responded to about 89 words and phrases; dogs don't think in sentences.
- EEG caps record electrical activity through the scalp with dozens of electrodes; they show broad patterns, not words.
- A 5-year-old lost in a crowd: police advice is to stay put and look for help; most lost children are found quickly.
- Crowd maths: a school of 1,000 people x about 6,200 thoughts a day = about 6 million thoughts a day around you. A football stadium can hold about 60,000 people.

### cold

NARRATOR: You wake up, and your mum pops her head round the door. Morning, love!
[pause 0.4]
NARRATOR: But that's not all you hear. Right behind her smile, clear as a voice, you hear: I hope they didn't notice I ate their chocolate.

### dog
*The kitchen. The dog stares at you. Its thought bubble: pictures, not words. A ball. A lead. A sausage.*
NARRATOR: Downstairs, it's the dog's turn. Dogs don't think in sentences. One study found the average dog understands about eighty nine words and phrases. So what you hear is more like a feeling. Walk? Walk. Sausage? Walk.
[pause 0.3]
NARRATOR: You give him a sausage. He thinks you're a genius.
[hold 0.5]

### bus
*The school bus. A dozen passengers, each with a thought bubble. The driver: "Twelve more years till I retire."*
NARRATOR: On the bus, everyone is quiet. Out loud, anyway. The driver is counting the years to retirement. The woman next to you is practising a job interview in her head. The man behind you is singing the same four words of a song, over and over, and it's driving you mad.
[hold 0.5]

### school
*School gates. Hundreds of kids. Thought bubbles everywhere, overlapping. You grab your head.*
NARRATOR: At school, it gets loud. Not the corridor. The thinking. Three hundred kids, all thinking at once.
[pause 0.3]
NARRATOR: You can read minds. Anyone within about ten metres, whether you want to or not.
[hold 0.8]

### rules
*Three rules on screen as icons: 10 M RANGE. LOUDER WHEN THEY FEEL MORE. NO OFF SWITCH. Then, through a wall, a sleeping brother's dream bubble: a giant sandwich chasing him.*
NARRATOR: You work out the rules in the first week. It reaches about ten metres. Strong feelings are loudest; fear is a shout, boredom is a mumble. And there's no off switch.
[pause 0.3]
NARRATOR: Not even at night. Through the wall, you can hear your little brother dreaming. Tonight, he's being chased by a giant sandwich.
[hold 0.6]

### brain
*A glowing brain, sparks running across it. 86 BILLION NEURONS. ≈ 20 WATTS.*
NARRATOR: Here's what you're listening to. Your brain has about eighty six billion neurons. They talk to each other with tiny electrical pulses, and the whole thing runs on about twenty watts. Less than a light bulb.
[pause 0.3]
NARRATOR: A thought isn't one neuron. It's a pattern, millions of them firing together, like a city's lights flickering in a particular shape.
[hold 0.6]

### energy
*The brain on a scale against a body. 2% OF YOUR WEIGHT. 20% OF YOUR ENERGY.*
NARRATOR: For something so small, it's hungry. Your brain is only about two percent of your body weight, but it burns around a fifth of your energy. Which might explain why, after a day of hearing everyone else's thoughts on top of your own, you're starving.
[hold 0.5]

### decoder
*A lab: a person in an MRI scanner. On a screen, words appear, roughly. UT AUSTIN, 2023. 16 HOURS IN THE SCANNER.*
NARRATOR: And scientists can already read some of it. In two thousand and twenty three, a team at the University of Texas built a decoder that turned brain scans into the rough meaning of what someone was hearing or imagining.
[pause 0.3]
NARRATOR: But each person had to lie in a scanner for about sixteen hours to train it. It got the gist, not the exact words. And it only worked if they wanted it to.
[pause 0.3]
NARRATOR: You? No scanner. Exact words. And nobody gets asked.
[hold 0.8]

### test
*Maths test. Above every head, a thought: "No idea." Above the teacher: the answers, in order.*
NARRATOR: Maths test. Thirty kids, and twenty nine of them are thinking the same thing. I have no idea.
[pause 0.3]
NARRATOR: But the teacher made the test. And while she marks last week's homework, she's mentally checking her answer key. Question one, forty two. Question two, seven point five.
[hold 0.5]

### honest
*You put your pen down. You do it yourself. A B+. You grin anyway.*
NARRATOR: You could copy every answer. You don't. Mostly because you'd never be able to explain how you got a hundred percent without showing your working.
[hold 0.5]

### poker
*Game night. Friends around a table with crisps as chips. Thought bubbles: "Pair of threes." "Bluffing, bluffing, bluffing."*
NARRATOR: Game night. Your friends play poker for crisps. You hear every hand. Pair of threes. Nothing, but act confident. Bluffing, bluffing, bluffing.
[pause 0.3]
NARRATOR: By ten o'clock, every bag of crisps in the house is on your side of the table. You stop inviting yourself to game night after that.
[hold 0.5]

### penalty
*Football. You're in goal. The striker's thought: "Bottom left. Bottom left." You dive bottom left.*
NARRATOR: Penalties. The striker runs up, and his head is shouting: bottom left, bottom left, bottom left. You dive bottom left before he's even kicked it. Seven penalties. Seven saves. Nobody can work out how.
[hold 0.5]

### gifts
*Birthday. Your friend opens your present and their thought bubble and face light up together: exactly what they wanted.*
NARRATOR: Birthdays get easy. You know exactly what everyone wants, because they think about it every time they walk past a shop window. Your gifts are perfect. Every single time.
[hold 0.5]

### bike
*A front garden. A used bike for sale. The seller says "Sixty, no less." His thought: "I'd take forty."*
NARRATOR: You want a second hand bike. The seller crosses his arms. Sixty pounds, he says. Not a penny less.
[pause 0.3]
NARRATOR: His head says: honestly, I'd take forty, I just want it out of the garden. You offer forty. He shakes your hand so fast it's almost rude.
[hold 0.5]

### pins
*A shop queue. People tapping cards. Their thought bubbles: 4-digit numbers. You look away, hands in pockets.*
NARRATOR: And then there are the things you really shouldn't hear. In the shop queue, people think their PIN numbers as they type them. Every single one. You stare at the ceiling, hum a song, and promise yourself you will never, ever use them.
[hold 0.5]

### party
*Your friends whispering, hiding balloons. Thought bubbles: "SURPRISE PARTY. Saturday. Don't let them find out."*
NARRATOR: Some things get ruined. Your friends spend three weeks planning a surprise party for you. You hear every detail. Every time. When they jump out and shout surprise, you have to act shocked, and you've been practising your face in the mirror for days.
[hold 0.5]

### crush
*Lunch hall. A kid at another table glances at you. Thought bubble: a little heart.*
NARRATOR: And then, at lunch, you hear something you weren't expecting. Someone at the next table glances over at you, and thinks: I hope they sit here.
[pause 0.3]
NARRATOR: You sit there. It goes surprisingly well.
[hold 0.6]

### quiz
*A TV quiz show. Bright lights. You at a podium. The host's thought: the answer. A giant cheque.*
NARRATOR: Then you go bigger. A TV quiz show. The host reads the question from a card, and you hear the answer printed on the back of it before he's finished asking.
[pause 0.3]
NARRATOR: Ten in a row. By question eight, the host is thinking: please get one wrong, we're nearly out of budget. You don't. The studio goes wild. You walk away with five thousand pounds.
[hold 0.5]

### police
*A police station. Two detectives. An interview room. A man saying "I wasn't there" while his thought bubble says "the bag's under the bridge."*
NARRATOR: A detective hears about the quiz. She asks if you'll sit in on an interview, just to watch. The man across the table says, I was home all night.
[pause 0.3]
NARRATOR: But in his head: the bag's under the bridge. They find it in twenty minutes.
[pause 0.3]
NARRATOR: Normal lie detectors can't do that. A polygraph only measures heart rate, breathing and sweat. Signs of stress, not lies. Lots of scientists don't trust them at all. You don't need one.
[hold 0.6]

### eeg
*A university lab. You wearing an EEG cap with wires. Scientists staring at squiggly lines on a monitor.*
NARRATOR: Scientists hear about you too. They put an EEG cap on your head, dozens of little sensors that pick up electrical signals through your scalp. On the screen, just squiggly lines, the same as everyone else's. They can see that your brain is busy. They can't see why. You don't tell them.
[hold 0.5]

### noise
*Your head among a crowd. Thought bubbles stacking until the screen is full of them. You on the floor.*
NARRATOR: But it never switches off. One study estimated people have about six thousand two hundred thoughts a day. Your school has a thousand people in it. That's around six million thoughts a day, all around you, all the time.
[pause 0.3]
NARRATOR: And at a football match, it's worse. Sixty thousand people, all thinking at once, about the referee mostly. You last eleven minutes.
[pause 0.3]
NARRATOR: The headaches start in week two. By week four, you eat lunch alone in the music room, just because it's quiet.
[hold 0.6]

### truth
*Your best friend laughing at your joke. Their thought bubble: "Not funny. But I'll laugh."*
NARRATOR: And then there's the truth. Your best friend laughs at your joke. And thinks, not funny, but I'll laugh anyway.
[pause 0.3]
NARRATOR: It's not even mean. It's kind. But you can never not know. Every smile comes with subtitles.
[hold 0.6]

### mum
*Night. Your mum at the kitchen table, worried. Thought bubble: bills, numbers.*
NARRATOR: Some things are worse. You hear your mum worrying about money, at two in the morning, when she thinks everyone's asleep. She smiles at breakfast like nothing's wrong. You know better.
[hold 0.8]

### help
*Morning. The kitchen table. An envelope with the quiz cheque, and a note: "For the bills."*
NARRATOR: So one morning, you leave an envelope on the kitchen table. The quiz money, and a note. She doesn't say anything about it. But at breakfast, for the first time in weeks, the voice in her head is quiet.
[hold 0.6]

### fair
*A summer fair. Huge crowd, rides, music. A mother shouting a name. LOST: 5 YEARS OLD.*
NARRATOR: Then, one summer afternoon, at the town fair, a woman starts screaming a name. Her little girl. Five years old. Gone. There are about twenty thousand people here.
[hold 0.4]

### listen
*You close your eyes. Thousands of thought bubbles roar. You push through the noise, looking for one small voice.*
NARRATOR: You close your eyes, and you do the thing you've spent months trying not to do. You listen to everyone.
[pause 0.3]
NARRATOR: Twenty thousand minds hit you at once. Hot dogs. Phones. Rides. Lost my car keys. It's like standing inside a stadium full of people shouting.
[pause 0.3]
NARRATOR: And somewhere under all of it, very small: I want mummy.
[hold 0.6]

### search
*You walk through the crowd, eyes half closed, turning your head like a radio dial. The small voice gets louder.*
NARRATOR: You can't tell where it's coming from. So you walk. Left, the voice gets quieter. Right, a little louder. You follow it like the dial on an old radio.
[pause 0.3]
NARRATOR: Past the hook a duck. Past the bumper cars. Past a man thinking very hard about whether he locked his car. Your head is pounding. Your nose starts to bleed. Louder.
[pause 0.3]
NARRATOR: Then a crowd blocks the way, and the voice is drowned out completely. You stand still, breathe, and wait. Three seconds. Five. There. Behind the ghost train.
[hold 0.4]

### found
*Behind a generator, a little girl crying, hiding. You kneel down. She looks up.*
NARRATOR: Behind a generator, behind the ghost train, she's sitting on the grass, crying, too scared to come out. You kneel down. She doesn't know you. But you know exactly what to say, because you can hear exactly what she's afraid of.
[pause 0.3]
NARRATOR: You walk her back. Her mum runs at you so fast she nearly knocks you both over.
[hold 1.0]

### quiet
*The fair at sunset. You sit alone on a bench. For the first time, the thoughts around you feel warm, not loud.*
NARRATOR: That night, for the first time in months, all the noise feels a little bit warmer.
[hold 0.8]

### twist
*Next day. A new kid joins your class. Above everyone's head, thoughts. Above theirs: nothing. Total silence. They turn and look at you.*
NARRATOR: The next morning, there's a new kid in your class. And above everyone's head, the same old noise.
[pause 0.3]
NARRATOR: Except theirs. Complete silence. Nothing at all.
[pause 0.5]
NARRATOR: They turn around, look straight at you, and you hear one thought, loud and clear, that isn't yours.
[pause 0.4]
NARRATOR: I can hear you too.
[pause 0.6]
NARRATOR: And then, for the first time in your life, you realise how loud your own thoughts must be.
[hold 1.6]
