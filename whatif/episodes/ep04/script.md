# Episode 4: What If You Shrank to the Size of an Ant?

One continuous story about YOU, present tense: the fun of being tiny, the real science of why small is different (falls, strength, heat, voice, water, wind, rain), the cost (cold, hungry, unheard, hunted by the cat), a climax in a flood where you use what you've learned and the ants save you, a twist, then a question for the comments. One narrator (Kokoro "Heart"). Speech appears on screen; nobody else speaks out loud.

How it's drawn: the camera lives at ant height. You stay the usual size on screen and the world is built five hundred times bigger: carpet fibres like trees, a cornflake like a bus, Mum's slipper like a bus parking. Pip the ant is about pony-sized next to you.

Scale used: a 1.5 m child shrunk to 3 mm is 500 times smaller. A 4 m bedroom feels like 2 km; a 50 cm bed feels 250 m high (a skyscraper); a 2.5 cm cornflake feels 12 m long (a bus); a 4 mm raindrop or water drop feels 2 m across (a car); a 7 mm ladybird feels 3.5 m long (a car).

Facts used, and where they come from:
- J.B.S. Haldane, "On Being the Right Size" (Harper's Magazine, 1926): "You can drop a mouse down a thousand-yard mine shaft; and, on arriving at the bottom, it gets a slight shock and walks away." Why: weight falls with volume (size cubed), air resistance with area (size squared), so small things fall slowly.
- The square-cube law (Galileo, Two New Sciences, 1638). Muscle strength goes with cross-section (size squared), weight with volume (size cubed). Shrink 500 times: strength down 250,000 times, weight down 125,000,000 times, so strength for your weight is 500 times greater.
- Schultheiss, Nooten and colleagues, PNAS, 2022: about 20 quadrillion (20 × 10^15) ants on Earth (about 2.5 million per person for 8 billion people); their dry carbon biomass, about 12 megatons, is more than all wild birds and wild mammals combined.
- Nguyen, Lilly and Castro, Ohio State University, Journal of Biomechanics, 2014: the neck joints of Allegheny mound ants withstood loads of about 3,400 to 5,000 times the ant's body weight before failing.
- Ants follow scent (pheromone) trails laid by other ants.
- Etruscan shrew, one of the smallest mammals (about 2 g): the highest heart rate recorded was 1,511 beats a minute (Jürgens and colleagues, Journal of Experimental Biology, 1996); it eats more than its own weight each day. Small warm bodies lose heat fast (more surface for their volume); no warm-blooded animal is anywhere near ant size. Ants don't make their own body heat.
- Smaller vocal folds vibrate faster, so the voice gets higher. Holy and Guo, Washington University in St. Louis, PLoS Biology, 2005: male mice sing songs in the 30 to 110 kHz range, above human hearing (about 20 kHz).
- Surface tension makes water sticky at small sizes. Gao and Jiang, Nature, 2004: a water strider's leg, covered in tiny water-repelling hairs, can hold up about 15 times the insect's body weight.
- Cummins and colleagues, University of Edinburgh, Nature, 2018: air flowing through a dandelion seed's bristles makes a "separated vortex ring" above it that slows its fall.
- Dickerson and colleagues, Georgia Tech, PNAS, 2012: mosquitoes survive raindrops that can weigh up to 50 times more than them; they're so light the drop barely slows, carries them briefly, and they peel off.
- Mlot, Tovey and Hu, Georgia Tech, PNAS, 2011: fire ants link legs and jaws into waterproof rafts; air trapped among their bodies keeps them afloat for days or weeks until floods subside.

### haldane

### cube
*A big cube and a tiny cube. MUSCLE STRENGTH ÷ 250,000. WEIGHT ÷ 125,000,000. RESULT: 500× STRONGER FOR YOUR WEIGHT. GALILEO · 1638.*
NARRATOR: The reason is simple maths. How strong a muscle is depends on how thick it is. How heavy you are depends on all of you.
[pause 0.3]
NARRATOR: Shrink five hundred times, and your muscles get two hundred and fifty thousand times weaker. But your weight drops a hundred and twenty five million times. So for your size, you're now five hundred times stronger.
[pause 0.3]
NARRATOR: Galileo worked out the rule behind this, nearly four hundred years ago. Ants have the same advantage, and they use it.
