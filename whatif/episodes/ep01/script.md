# Episode 1: What If You Could Stop Time?

How this channel tells a story (learned from the top "What if" channels): one continuous story about YOU, in the present tense. Every scene escalates. Real numbers are dropped into the moment, never as a lecture. The power has a cost. A big rescue at the climax uses everything learned. A twist ending. Then a question for the comments.
One narrator (Kokoro "Heart"). Nobody else speaks out loud; anything they say is on screen. About 10 minutes.

Facts used (and the maths behind them):
- Light: about 300,000 km a second. You see because light keeps arriving at your eyes. (Stopping time is a thought experiment; the script says what physicists usually reason would happen.)
- Air molecules at room temperature: roughly 500 m a second on average. Sound is air vibrating.
- Slowed 1,000 times: half a second (bus doors) = 500 s, about 8 minutes. A 30-minute lunch = 30,000 minutes, about 3 weeks. A dodgeball at about 15 m/s = 1.5 cm/s.
- Hand waved at 2 m/s in slow time = 2,000 m/s in the real world, about Mach 6 (speed of sound about 343 m/s). Air hitting something that fast heats up sharply.
- Human reaction time: about a quarter of a second. Slowed 1,000 times: about 4 minutes.
- Usain Bolt's 100 m world record average: about 10.4 m/s. Shuffling 1 cm/s in slow time = 10 m/s real. 100 m at 10 m/s = 10 s real = 10,000 s of your time, about 2 hours 47 minutes.
- Raindrop: about 9 m/s, so 9 mm/s slowed; a garden snail manages about 1 cm/s.
- Hummingbird: about 50 wingbeats a second, so one every 20 seconds slowed. A voice: about 200 vibrations a second, so 0.2 slowed, far below the 20 a second human hearing starts at.
- MIT, 2011: a camera at about a trillion frames a second filmed a pulse of light crossing a bottle.
- Ageing: 15 minutes a day slowed = 15,000 minutes = about 10.4 of your days every day = about 10 extra years per real year.
- A phone falling from a table (about 1 m) takes about 0.45 s, so about 7.5 minutes slowed.
- A boiled egg takes about 10 minutes, so about 10,000 minutes, nearly 7 days, slowed.
- A housefly beats its wings about 200 times a second, so about once every 5 seconds slowed.
- A hard penalty kick: about 30 m/s, so 3 cm/s slowed.
- A lightning flash usually lasts around a fifth of a second, flickering through several strokes, so over 3 minutes slowed.
- People need roughly 8 hours of sleep for every day they're awake, so a 3-week lunch break means about 21 nights of sleep.
- The rescue: a car at 50 km/h = 13.9 m/s, about 15 m away, so about 1.1 s. Slowed: 1.4 cm/s, and about 18 minutes of your time. Moving a child 1 m in that real second is a gentle 1 m/s; done any faster it would hurt them, so you move them about 1 mm a second, for about 16 minutes.

### cold
*Rain. A city street at night. You're sprinting for the bus; the doors are closing.*
NARRATOR: You're late. The bus doors are closing. You snap your fingers, and the whole world stops.
[hold 0.4]

### frozen
*Raindrops hang in the air. A splash stops mid-air. A pigeon is frozen mid-flap. You grin.*
NARRATOR: The rain hangs in the air like glass beads. A pigeon freezes in mid-flap. Nobody moves but you.
[pause 0.5]
NARRATOR: Then everything goes black. And you can't breathe.
[hold 0.6]

### black
*Total darkness. Your eyes, wide, the only things lit. Then a glimpse: light frozen in mid-air, short of your face.*
NARRATOR: You see because light is always pouring into your eyes, about three hundred thousand kilometres every second. Stop time, and the light stops too. And the air? Air flows into your lungs because its molecules are always zooming around, about five hundred metres a second. Freeze them, and they just sit there.
[hold 0.4]

### gasp
*Snap. Colour and sound rush back. You're on your knees by the bus stop, gasping. The bus pulls away.*
NARRATOR: You snap your fingers again. You're on your knees, gasping, soaked. The bus is gone.
[pause 0.4]
NARRATOR: So you practise. And on the third night, you find the trick. Don't stop time. Slow it down. A thousand times.
[hold 0.8]

### rules
*The power, explained in three pictures: light still reaches your eyes, air still moves, a slow clock. 1,000x SLOWER.*
NARRATOR: Now the air still moves, just slowly, so you can breathe. Somehow, light still gets through, so you can see. Don't ask how. Physicists would have a lot of questions. But everything around you crawls.
[hold 0.4]

### tests
*Your bedroom. You test it: you knock a phone off the desk. Snap. It hangs halfway to the floor. You walk round it, look at it from below, then catch it.*
NARRATOR: First, you test it. You knock your phone off the desk. Snap. A phone falling from a desk takes about half a second to hit the floor. In slow time, that's about seven and a half minutes. You walk round it. You look at it from underneath. You make a sandwich. Then you come back and catch it.
[pause 0.3]
NARRATOR: You do it again. And again. By midnight, you've caught it two hundred times, and it has never once touched the floor.
[hold 0.5]

### egg
*The kitchen. You put an egg on to boil, then snap. The bubbles hang in the water.*
NARRATOR: Then you get hungry. Bad idea. In slow time, everything cooks slowly too. A boiled egg takes about ten minutes. Slowed a thousand times, that's nearly a week. The bubbles in the pan just sit there, like little glass marbles. You snap back and eat cereal instead.
[hold 0.5]

### bus
*Morning. The bus doors closing. Snap. They're barely moving. You stroll on board.*
NARRATOR: Next morning, the bus doors start to close. Snap. To you, those doors now take eight whole minutes to shut. You stroll on, sit down, and snap back.
[hold 0.5]

### test
*School. A test tomorrow. Lunch break. Snap. You read a pile of books taller than you.*
NARRATOR: Maths test tomorrow? A thirty minute lunch break becomes about three weeks of your time. You read the whole textbook. Twice. You score a hundred. Your teacher checks your sleeves for notes.
[hold 0.5]

### dodge
*PE. Dodgeball. A ball flies at your face. Snap. It hangs there. You step aside.*
NARRATOR: Dodgeball. A ball comes at your face at fifteen metres a second. To you, it's drifting at one and a half centimetres a second. You just step out of the way. Every throw. Every game.
[hold 0.5]

### penalty
*Football. A penalty. You're in goal. The striker shoots. Snap. The ball hangs in the air. You lean, gently, and catch it.*
NARRATOR: Then football. Your friends put you in goal for a joke. A hard penalty flies at about thirty metres a second. To you, that's three centimetres a second. You take your time, lean across, and pick it out of the air like an apple off a tree. Ten penalties. Ten saves. The striker sits down on the grass.
[hold 0.5]

### rps
*A school rock, paper, scissors tournament. Snap. The other kid's hand, halfway between rock and paper.*
NARRATOR: There's a rock, paper, scissors tournament at school. Most people's hands give them away about a quarter of a second before they finish. For you, that's four whole minutes to watch their fingers move. You win fifty games in a row. They ban you from the next one.
[hold 0.5]

### fly
*The playground. Older kids laugh: "Bet you can't catch a fly." Snap. A fly, its wings mid-beat. You pinch it gently out of the air.*
NARRATOR: Some older kids bet you five pounds you can't catch a fly. A housefly beats its wings about two hundred times a second. In slow time, it's one flap every five seconds, like a tiny helicopter that's run out of fuel. You walk up, take it gently between two fingers, snap back, and let it go out of the window. Five pounds. Each.
[hold 0.5]

### highfive
*Your friend holds up a hand for a high five. You're still in slow time. Your hand flies. The air in front of it glows. You yank it back, palm red.*
NARRATOR: Then your friend goes for a high five. You're still in slow time, and you swing your hand like normal.
[pause 0.3]
NARRATOR: Big mistake. To you, that's two metres a second. To the real world, it's two thousand. Six times the speed of sound. The air can't get out of the way. It slams into your palm and heats up like a meteor.
[hold 0.6]

### careful
*Your hand bandaged. You moving in slow time like a sloth, very carefully.*
NARRATOR: Your hand is blistered for a week. From then on, in slow time, you move like a sloth. Gently. Always.
[hold 0.5]

### teleport
*Lunch hall. You snap, walk across the room, snap back. Your friends see you vanish from one seat and appear in another. Speech bubble: "HOW?!"*
NARRATOR: There's another problem. To everyone else, you teleport. One second you're next to them, the next you're across the room holding a cookie. Your friend drops his fork. You start leaving one second gaps, walking very normally, and pretending you were there all along.
[hold 0.5]

### games
*Gaming. You at the top of a ranked leaderboard. Chat: CHEATER?*
NARRATOR: Video games. Most people react in about a quarter of a second. You get about four minutes to think about every single move. One month later, you're number one on the whole leaderboard. Half the chat thinks you're hacking.
[hold 0.5]

### magic
*The high street. A small crowd. You do card tricks; cards appear in people's pockets. A hat full of coins.*
NARRATOR: Then the high street. You set up a little table and do card tricks. The card someone picked ends up in their own back pocket. Their phone ends up in their friend's hand. Nobody sees you move. In one Saturday afternoon, the hat fills up with nearly two hundred pounds in coins.
[hold 0.5]

### race
*Sports day. 100 metres. Snap. You shuffle, centimetre by centimetre. The finish line, the crowd frozen.*
NARRATOR: Sports day. A hundred metre race. You can't run in slow time, so you shuffle. One centimetre a second. But to the real world, that's ten metres a second. Usain Bolt speed.
[pause 0.3]
NARRATOR: You cross the line in about ten seconds. For you, that race took nearly three hours of shuffling.
[hold 0.6]

### quiet
*Slow time at night. Rain drifting down slowly. A hummingbird's wings, one slow beat. Your friend talking, but no sound.*
NARRATOR: Sometimes, you go into slow time just to look. Rain drifts down at about a snail's pace. A hummingbird beats its wings about fifty times a second. In slow time, it's one lazy flap every twenty seconds.
[pause 0.3]
NARRATOR: And it's silent. A voice vibrates about two hundred times a second. Slow it down a thousand times, and it's far too low for any ear to hear. Your friends' voices just roll past you, as invisible waves.
[hold 0.6]

### lightning
*A storm. A lightning bolt flickers, frozen, branching across the sky. You stand on the roof, watching.*
NARRATOR: One night there's a storm. A lightning flash usually lasts about a fifth of a second. It flickers, several times, faster than you can see. In slow time, you sit on the roof and watch one bolt crawl across the sky, branch by branch, for over three minutes.
[hold 0.6]

### famous
*Your videos: impossible catches. Views counter climbing. A lab. A scientist in a white coat.*
NARRATOR: Somebody films you catching a falling phone from across the room. Then a glass. Then a toddler's ice cream. The clip hits fifty million views.
[pause 0.3]
NARRATOR: Then a morning TV show. The host drops a ball, a cup, a whole tray of cutlery, and you catch every piece before it lands. The studio audience screams.
[pause 0.3]
NARRATOR: A university calls. Their scientists have already done something like this with a camera. In two thousand and eleven, a team at MIT filmed a pulse of light crossing a bottle, at about a trillion frames a second. They want to know how your eyes do it without one.
[hold 0.6]

### cost
*A mirror. A calendar flipping. You, looking tired. Your friends haven't changed.*
NARRATOR: But there's a cost nobody sees. Every minute you spend in slow time, you live about sixteen hours. Fifteen minutes a day, and you're living an extra ten days, every day.
[pause 0.3]
NARRATOR: After a year, your friends are a year older. You're more like eleven.
[hold 0.6]

### sleep
*Lunch break in slow time. You, curled up asleep under a desk. A clock that barely moves. Z's.*
NARRATOR: And you still get tired. People need about eight hours of sleep for every day they're awake. So that three week lunch break you used to study? You had to sleep about twenty one times in it. On the classroom floor. While the rest of your class sat frozen, mid-sandwich.
[hold 0.6]

### lonely
*You alone in a frozen crowd. Everyone else a statue.*
NARRATOR: And it's lonely. Weeks of your life where nobody moves, and nobody talks. You catch yourself in a shop window. You look older than you should. You're fourteen.
[hold 0.8]

### drift
*Your best friend talking to you; you, distracted, a little older-looking. A birthday cake with candles.*
NARRATOR: You forget things your friends said, because for you it was weeks ago. You miss jokes. At your best friend's birthday, you've lived through a whole extra summer since the last one. He's the same. You're not.
[hold 0.6]

### doctor
*A doctor's room. The doctor frowning at a chart. Your age, and a second, bigger number.*
NARRATOR: At a check-up, the doctor frowns at your chart. Your height, your teeth, your bones. Everything says you're older than the date on your birth certificate. She checks it twice. You don't tell her why.
[hold 0.6]

### street
*An afternoon street. A small kid chases a ball into the road. A car, 15 metres away.*
NARRATOR: Then one afternoon, a little kid chases a ball into the road. A car is coming, about fifty kilometres an hour. Fifteen metres away. In about one second, it hits him.
[hold 0.3]

### snap
*Snap. Everything freezes. The car's front wheels, barely moving. You, twenty metres away.*
NARRATOR: Snap. The car is still moving, but now it's crawling, about one and a half centimetres a second. You've got around eighteen minutes of your time. And you're twenty metres away.
[hold 0.4]

### driver
*Close on the driver, frozen, looking at a phone. The kid, frozen mid-step, reaching for the ball.*
NARRATOR: You look at the driver. He's looking down at his phone. He hasn't even seen the kid yet. The kid is mid-step, one hand stretched out for the ball. He has no idea.
[hold 0.4]

### dash
*You, desperate, wanting to run, then stopping yourself. Glowing air around your hand as a warning.*
NARRATOR: Every part of you wants to sprint. But you remember the high five. Run in slow time, and the air would burn you up before you got there. So you walk. Slowly. Carefully. Every step a few centimetres.
[hold 0.4]

### reach
*You reach the kid. The car's bumper is close. You start to grab him, then stop.*
NARRATOR: You get there with three minutes to spare. You reach for him, and freeze. If you yank him out at your speed, to him it's like being hit by a car anyway.
[pause 0.3]
NARRATOR: So you push. One millimetre a second. For sixteen minutes. Your arms burn. The bumper creeps closer.
[pause 0.3]
NARRATOR: Five minutes. Ten. The bumper is a hand's width away now. Your arms are shaking. You can't speed up, and you can't stop. Fourteen minutes. Fifteen.
[hold 0.6]

### miss
*Snap back. The car brakes and swerves. The kid is sitting on the pavement, confused but safe. The ball rolls under the car.*
NARRATOR: Snap. Brakes screech. The car stops right where he was standing. The kid is sitting on the pavement, confused, without a scratch. All he felt was a gentle push.
[pause 0.3]
NARRATOR: Nobody saw you move. To everyone else, you were just standing there.
[hold 1.0]

### twist
*That night. Rain again. You snap, for a quiet look. The world freezes. Across the street, someone else is still moving. They look straight at you.*
NARRATOR: That night, you slip into slow time, just to watch the rain. Everything stops. The drops hang in the air.
[pause 0.5]
NARRATOR: And across the street, somebody else is still moving. They turn, and look straight at you.
[pause 0.6]
NARRATOR: They smile. They lift one hand. And they snap their fingers.
