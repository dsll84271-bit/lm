# Episode 9: What If You Woke Up Inside a Video Game?

One continuous story about YOU, present tense: you wake up on a beach in Miami and find out it's an open-world video game. A health bar, a mini-map, people who repeat themselves, a world that only exists where you look, a sun that races across the sky. You learn the game's rules one by one (and what real life does instead), find out the player is coming back and the world will switch off, and escape through a glitch at the edge of the map. Then the twist. One narrator (Kokoro "Heart"). Speech appears on screen; nobody else speaks out loud.

Rules (from the Episode 4 retention graph):
- The promise in the first line, and an open loop (a countdown) in the first 20 seconds.
- No block of facts longer than about 15 seconds; one number at a time, shown happening.
- Something new every 20 to 30 seconds.
- Family-friendly: no weapons, no crime. Cars only explode in the cartoon sense; nobody gets hurt.

How it's drawn: an ORIGINAL game world set in a real place, Miami: a sandy beach, palm trees, pastel Art Deco hotels with neon signs along a beach-front road, lifeguard towers, a pier, a causeway over blue water. No logos, names, characters or look from any real game; the game on screen has no title. A game HUD in our own style: a heart health bar, a round mini-map, a mission marker, a countdown.

Facts used, and where they come from:
- Miami Beach's Architectural District (the "Art Deco District") has about 960 historic buildings, most built in the 1920s to 1940s (National Register of Historic Places listing; Wikipedia).
- Miami was incorporated on 28 July 1896 and is often called the only major American city founded by a woman, Julia Tuttle, the "Mother of Miami" (Florida Humanities; CBS News).
- NPC means "non-player character": a character run by the game's own rules, usually with a small set of recorded lines.
- Frustum culling: game engines skip drawing anything outside the camera's view, to save work (Unity and Unreal Engine documentation).
- Games show a new picture many times a second, usually 30 or 60 frames per second; films are traditionally 24.
- 3D game worlds are built from flat triangles (polygons).
- In GTA V, one in-game minute lasts two real seconds, so a whole day lasts 48 real minutes (GTA Wiki).
- The men's high jump world record is 2.45 m, by Javier Sotomayor in 1993, and still stands (World Athletics; Guinness World Records).
- Real cars very rarely explode in crashes: a fuel tank holds liquid, and an explosion needs vapour mixed with air plus a spark (Jalopnik; ScienceABC).
- A broken arm or wrist usually takes about six to eight weeks to heal (NHS guidance, via clinic sources).
- Games check collisions with simple invisible shapes ("hitboxes"), which is why players can sometimes slip through walls.
- GTA V has sold over 230 million copies (Take-Two, 2026). GTA VI's first trailer got 90,421,491 YouTube views in its first 24 hours, a Guinness World Record (Guinness World Records, 2023).
