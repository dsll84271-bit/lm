# Episode 8: What If You Woke Up 100 Metres Tall?

One continuous story about YOU, present tense: you wake up a hundred metres tall with your head through the roof, find out what that really means (your weight, your bones, your voice, the view, your blood, your food, your heat, your footsteps), feel what it costs, then save a school bus on a collapsing bridge in a storm. At night you shrink back… and keep shrinking, down to ant size, where an old friend is waiting (a link to Episode 4). One narrator (Kokoro "Heart"). Speech appears on screen; nobody else speaks out loud.

Rules for this episode, from the retention graph of Episode 4:
- The promise in the title is in the first line.
- No block of facts longer than about 15 seconds. One big number at a time, shown happening.
- Something new happens every 20 to 30 seconds: a place, a danger, or a joke.
- An open loop in the first 20 seconds: tonight, a whole bus of kids will need you.

How it's drawn: one world in real metres (a cartoon world: your house is a shell round your ankle, not a room you fit in). You are 100 m tall, so houses come up to your shins, cars are toys and people are crumbs. Two kinds of shot: GIANT VIEW (you, with the town as a model village around your feet) and STREET VIEW (normal people on the street, with your trainer, hand or face filling the sky).

Facts used, and where they come from:
- Elizabeth Tower (Big Ben) is 96 m tall (UK Parliament, Big Ben facts and figures).
- Grow about 60 times taller (1.6 m to 100 m) and, keeping your shape, your weight goes up 60 × 60 × 60, about 216,000 times: a 45 kg child becomes about 9,700 tonnes. The Eiffel Tower weighs about 10,100 tonnes in total (Eiffel Tower official key figures).
- J.B.S. Haldane, "On Being the Right Size" (1926): a giant 60 feet high, ten times taller than a man, would be a thousand times heavier but have bones only a hundred times stronger, and would break its thigh bones with every step.
- Elephant rumbles go below what human ears can hear (infrasound) and can carry up to about 10 km in good conditions (Elephant Listening Project, Cornell; ScienceNews).
- Horizon distance is about 3.57 × √(eye height in metres) km: at 1.5 m that's about 4.4 km; at about 94 m it's about 35 km.
- Giraffes have blood pressure roughly twice that of humans, to push blood about two metres up the neck to the brain (EarthDate; Discover Wildlife; Live Science).
- Bigger animals burn energy more slowly for their size (Kleiber's law, energy use rising with weight to the power three quarters): 216,000 times heavier means roughly 10,000 times more food. Elephants eat about 150 kg of plants a day and spend up to 16 to 18 hours a day eating (SeaWorld; San Diego Zoo).
- Big bodies hold heat: elephants send warm blood into their thin, vein-filled ears and flap them to cool down (Live Science; Popular Science).
- In July 2023, Taylor Swift's two Eras Tour concerts at Lumen Field in Seattle shook the ground as much as a magnitude 2.3 earthquake, measured by seismologist Jackie Caplan-Auerbach of Western Washington University (WWU; CBS News).
- Robert Wadlow (1918–1940) is the tallest person ever recorded, 2.72 m. He needed leg braces and a stick to walk; a badly fitted brace caused a blister on his ankle that became infected, and he died aged 22 (Guinness World Records).
