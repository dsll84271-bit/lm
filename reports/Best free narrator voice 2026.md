# Keep Qwen3-TTS, test two free challengers

As of late November 2026, no free option that is clearly licensed for a monetised channel and also runs on a 4-core, 15 GB, no-GPU machine sounds better than the channel's current setup: a Qwen3-TTS 1.7B voice made with VoiceDesign and cloned with the Base model. Don't switch. The voices that rank higher in blind tests fail one of the requirements. Fish Audio Drama 3, Step-Audio-EditX and Ming-3.0-tts top the open-weights arenas, but most are 3B to 35B parameters and won't run on this machine's CPU or fit in 6 GB of disk. Breeze TTS 2, Fish S2 Pro and Voxtral are non-commercial. ElevenLabs, the overall leader, has a free plan that bans commercial use. The strongest free challenger is Google's hosted **Gemini 3.8 Flash TTS**. Its predecessor outscored every open-weights model on the Artificial Analysis arena, and it costs nothing in AI Studio. But Google has not stated in writing that free-tier output may be used commercially, its current arena rank is unknown, and it can't carry over the channel's existing voice. The ranked shortlist is: (1) Qwen3-TTS 1.7B, which you already have and should keep; (2) Gemini 3.8 Flash TTS, worth an A/B test for a new voice; (3) Ming-3.0-tts 0.5B, the one open model that could beat Qwen at cloning but is unproven on CPU; (4) Google Cloud Chirp 3: HD, the most clearly licensed hosted option; and (5) Kokoro-82M, a fast fallback with a clean licence. YouTube does not demonetise videos for using an AI voice. Its "inauthentic content" rules target mass-produced, templated videos. For this channel, script originality and natural delivery matter more than which TTS model you use.

## The blind-test leaders are too big, non-commercial, or both

The best realism evidence available is crowd-voted Elo from the Artificial Analysis Speech Arena, and its boards disagree with each other and change month to month. The overall leaders are paid models. One November snapshot puts **Eleven v4 Turbo at about 1345 Elo**, followed by Eleven v4 and StepFun's StepAudio 3 TTS at 1315 ([Artificial Analysis](https://artificialanalysis.ai/text-to-speech/leaderboard/provider-voice)). Among open weights, the top spot has rotated during 2026. **Breeze TTS 2 took #1 at 1,215 Elo in August**, but under a research/non-commercial licence ([AlphaSignal](https://alphasignal.ai/news/breezeblue-s-breeze-tts-2-tops-open-weights-voice-ai-with-1-215-elo)). A later snapshot lists **Fish Audio Drama 3 at about 1278** ([Artificial Analysis open weights](https://artificialanalysis.ai/text-to-speech/leaderboard/provider-voice/open-weights)). A third-party board updated 15 November also puts Drama 3 first, at 1275 ([17nas](https://17nas.com/tts-leaderboard.php?lang=en-US)). The board closest to this channel's workflow is the **Controlled Voice arena**, where every model clones the same fixed voice. In October, Ant Group's **Ming-3.0-tts took #1 there at 1,069**, ahead of Drama 3 Preview at 1,059 ([Artificial Analysis on X](https://x.com/ArtificialAnlys/status/2112389514404462613)).

Each leader fails a requirement. **Fish Audio S2 Pro** weights state that "no commercial rights are granted" without a separate written agreement ([HF LICENSE](https://huggingface.co/fishaudio/s2-pro/blob/main/LICENSE.md)). **Voxtral TTS** is CC BY-NC ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.25551v1)). **Higgs Audio V3** is research-only ([Boson README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/boson-ai/higgs-audio/HEAD/README.md)). **IndexTTS-2** needs bilibili's prior written authorisation for commercial use, even though several sites call it Apache-2.0 ([index-tts LICENSE](https://github.com/index-tts/index-tts/blob/main/LICENSE)). **Fish Audio Drama 3** is reported to allow free commercial use below US$500k in annual revenue with a "Built with Fish Audio" notice ([eesel](https://www.eesel.ai/blog/fish-audio-drama-3-review)). But it is a **35B mixture-of-experts model**, and its Hugging Face tag still shows the research licence. **Step-Audio-EditX** is Apache-2.0 but has 3–4B parameters ([GitHub](https://github.com/stepfun-ai/Step-Audio-EditX)). Neither Drama 3 nor Step-Audio-EditX fits in 6 GB of free disk or runs at usable speed on four cores.

The hosted free tiers split the same way. The **ElevenLabs free plan "does not include a commercial license"** and requires "elevenlabs.io" in the video title ([ElevenLabs](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/help-center/legal/can-i-publish-the-content-i-generate-on-the-platform)). The free plans from Hume, Murf, PlayHT and Fish Audio are also non-commercial ([undetectr](https://undetectr.com/blog/ai-voice-generator-free); [smallest.ai](https://smallest.ai/blog/fish-audio-pricing-plans-api-billing-commercial-use-in-2026)). The free **edge-tts** route to Microsoft's voices has no published licence, and a Microsoft moderator said that using it commercially without an Azure subscription "could be a violation of our terms" ([Microsoft Q&A](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/answers/questions/6007729/commercial-use-of-microsoft-edge-text-to-speech-vi)).

### Qwen3-TTS 1.7B remains the best voice this machine can run

Qwen3-TTS is **Apache-2.0**, which covers commercial use of the model and of the speech it generates ([AI Game Dev](https://ai-game.dev/packages/aigd.audio-qwen3-tts-0-6b-base)). It is the most consistently recommended open model in 2026 roundups ([tryspeakeasy](https://www.tryspeakeasy.io/blog/open-source-text-to-speech-2026)). With Chatterbox retired, Cartesia calls Qwen3-TTS Base "the closest actively maintained, permissively licensed option for voice cloning" ([Cartesia](https://www.cartesia.ai/learn/open-source-tts)). The channel's design-then-clone workflow is the method the Qwen3-TTS README recommends. VoiceDesign produces a slightly different voice on every call, so you design the voice once, call `create_voice_clone_prompt(ref_audio, ref_text)` once, and reuse that prompt for every line ([Qwen3-TTS README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/QwenLM/Qwen3-TTS/HEAD/README.md)).

On CPU, the only measured figure is for the **0.6B int8 model: RTF about 3.07 on a 4-vCPU/15 GB machine**, roughly 25 minutes per 8 minutes of audio ([OCDevel](https://ocdevel.com/blog/20260711-quantized-cpu-tts)). Since 1.7B has about three times the parameters, a rough estimate is 50–80 minutes per episode, which fits a one-to-two-hour budget. The channel's own timings are better evidence than this estimate. The weights take roughly 3.4–4 GB at bf16. Once the reference clip exists, the VoiceDesign weights aren't needed, so deleting them frees disk space.

The main weakness is long-form drift. A September 2026 paper found that Qwen3-TTS and VoxCPM2 do very well on short prompts but **"significantly deteriorate" on long-form input** ([HF papers](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.16989.md)). Even a narrator fine-tune repo still chunks text at 300 characters or fewer for this reason ([GitHub](https://github.com/sabari245/qwen3-tts-audiobook-finetune)). Better chunking will improve the narration more than a different model would.

### Gemini 3.8 Flash TTS is the realism wildcard, with licence and voice caveats

Gemini 3.8 Flash TTS and Flash-Lite TTS became generally available on 22 September 2026, with voice design and voice replication ([Gemini changelog](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/changelog); [Gemini TTS docs](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/speech-generation)). Several sources say both are **free in AI Studio and on the API free tier** ([projedefteri](https://projedefteri.com/en/blog/is-gemini-3-8-tts-free/)). The realism case is indirect. In one Artificial Analysis snapshot, **Gemini 3.1 Flash TTS led the whole arena at 1214 Elo, while the best open model scored 1123** ([Artificial Analysis](https://artificialanalysis.ai/text-to-speech/leaderboard?tab=leaderboard)). No current rank for 3.8 turned up, and developers on Google's forum say **3.8 voices sound flatter than 3.1** ([Google AI forum](https://discuss.ai.google.dev/t/gemini-3-8-tts-voice-is-a-downgrade-for-personal-assistant-and-companion-apps/184911)).

On terms, Google "won't claim ownership" of generated output, and the service is aimed at "professional or business purposes". On the unpaid tier, though, prompts may be used for training and read by human reviewers ([Gemini API terms](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/terms)). No source quoted a clause that explicitly permits commercial use on the free tier, and secondary sources disagree ([terms.law](https://terms.law/ai-output-rights/gemini/); [freetier.wiki](https://freetier.wiki/tools/gemini-api/)).

Gemini also can't carry over the channel's voice cleanly. Voice replication requires a 30-second sample plus a spoken consent recording, and AI Studio doesn't offer it in the EEA, UK or Switzerland ([arwriterai](https://arwriterai.com/en/blog/gemini-3-8-flash-tts-voice-studio-2026/)). The channel's voice was designed by a model, so there is no real person to record that consent. Switching to Gemini means adopting a new voice, either one of its 2,000+ prompt-shaped voices ([mer.vin](https://mer.vin/news/gemini-3-8-tts-2000-voices-and-native-two-speaker-dialogue/)) or one designed to resemble the current narrator. Free-tier limits are visible only inside your AI Studio project ([nemovideo](https://www.nemovideo.com/blog/gemini-3-1-flash-tts-pricing)). The 2.5 and 3.1 preview models are being retired around 17 November 2026, though one guide gives 18 December ([deprecations](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/deprecations)), so use `gemini-3.8-flash-tts`.

### Ming-3.0-tts is the only open model that might beat Qwen at cloning

Ming-3.0-tts comes in a 0.5B dense model and a 7.9B-A1.3B mixture-of-experts model. It supports cloning, voice design and control through natural-language instructions. The maintainers confirmed in a GitHub issue that the weights are **MIT and commercially usable, including for consented cloning**, although at the time the weights repo was missing its LICENSE file ([Issue #19](https://github.com/inclusionAI/Ming-3.0-tts/issues/19)). Its #1 Controlled Voice ranking measures exactly what this channel does. But the sources don't say which size was ranked, one roundup lists Ming as a GPU model ([OCDevel](https://ocdevel.com/blog/20250720-tts)), and the local-inference code was released after the weights ([kie.ai](https://kie.ai/blog/what-is-ming-3-0)). At 0.5B, its weights would take roughly 1 GB, and it should run at about Qwen 0.6B's speed if the code supports CPU at all. It is worth an afternoon's test: clone the existing Qwen reference clip and compare the results blind.

### Chirp 3: HD and Kokoro are the safe fallbacks

**Google Cloud Text-to-Speech gives 1 million free Chirp 3: HD characters a month**, then charges $30 per million ([Google Cloud pricing](https://cloud.google.com/text-to-speech/pricing?hl=en)). An 8-minute script is about 7,000 characters, so the free allowance covers more than 100 episodes a month. Because it is a standard paid Cloud service, its commercial terms are clear. It requires a billing account, so set a budget alert. **Azure Speech F0** is a comparable option, with 500,000 free neural characters a month, but its HD voices are reportedly excluded ([texttolab](https://texttolab.com/blog/azure-text-to-speech-pricing)). **Speechify's developer API** says its free plan "includes 500K characters a month with commercial use" ([Speechify](https://speechify.ai/text-to-speech-api)), but other Speechify pages list 50K, so screenshot the terms when you sign up.

**Kokoro-82M** is Apache-2.0 and runs at **RTF 0.57 (ONNX) to 0.79 (PyTorch) on CPU** ([heyneo](https://heyneo.com/blog/kokoro-tts-vs-supertonic-3-tts)), so a full episode takes minutes. It scored MOS 4.44 against Pocket TTS's 4.10 ([heyneo](https://heyneo.com/blog/kokoro-supertonic-inflect-nano-pocket-tts-cpu-benchmark)). It has only preset voices and can't clone, and its prosody goes flat on expressive material ([spokio](https://spokio.pro/tts-for-audiobooks-long-form-narration)). Expect a moody detective read to sound less acted than it does with Qwen.

**Chatterbox Turbo** (MIT) narrowly missed the list. A GGML port runs at 1.2–1.7× real time on a 16-thread CPU using 2.5 GB of memory ([AI Game Dev](https://ai-game.dev/packages/aigd.tts-chatterbox-turbo)). But Resemble retired Chatterbox commercially in August 2026 and stopped updating it ([Resemble](https://www.resemble.ai/chatterbox-turbo/?id=ChatterboxTurbo)).

## Human-sounding narration comes from the reference, the chunking and the gaps

Most of a clone's quality comes from the reference clip. Use **10–15 seconds of clean, uncompressed, single-speaker audio with an exact transcript** ([OCDevel](https://ocdevel.com/blog/20260302-qwen-tts-voice-cloning)), and run Qwen's ICL mode (`x_vector_only_mode=False` with `ref_text`), which the docs "strongly recommend" over embedding-only cloning ([Qwen3-TTS docs](https://mintlify.com/QwenLM/Qwen3-TTS/guides/voice-cloning)). Models copy whatever is in the clip, including codec artifacts from a compressed recording ([El Farssi](https://amineelfarssi.github.io/blog/voice-cloning-f5-tts-experiment/)). The clip also sets the delivery, so generate the VoiceDesign reference as a low, measured, noir-style read rather than a neutral announcer read, and every later line will inherit that style.

For long narration, build the clone prompt once and reuse it for every chunk, because mixing prompts causes audible seams ([Speechify](https://speechify.ai/blog/building-an-automated-audiobook-pipeline-with-the-speechify-tts-api)). Split by paragraph first, then by sentence, and never mid-sentence. Generate one to three sentences at a time and render two or three takes of each chunk to catch glitches. Chunking improves accuracy within each chunk but loses continuity across chunks, and standard metrics don't detect that loss ([Emilia2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.13614)). Close the gap by hand: use **roughly 250–400 ms between sentences and 600–900 ms at paragraph breaks** ([voicebox #363](https://github.com/jamiepine/voicebox/issues/363)), vary those lengths so the rhythm doesn't sound mechanical, and listen back paragraph by paragraph, because sentence-level checks miss drift in intonation ([Bland.ai](https://www.bland.ai/blog/how-to-make-text-to-speech-sound-more-natural)). Write the script for **about 130–150 words per minute**, which for 8 minutes is roughly 1,050–1,200 words ([StudioBinder](https://www.studiobinder.com/blog/narration-in-documentary/); [Typecast](https://typecast.ai/learn/?p=15068)).

In post-production, keep the processing light. Clean up the audio, apply subtractive EQ around 300–500 Hz to remove boxiness, de-ess lightly before compressing, and compress gently at 2:1 to 4:1 ([Voice.ai](https://voice.ai/hub/tts/how-to-make-text-to-speech-sound-less-robotic/); [Slate Digital](https://slatedigital.com/how-to-build-the-perfect-vocal-chain-with-plugins/)). Add a faint room tone or reverb so the voice doesn't sound bone-dry over the music. Skip hard noise gates, which clip word endings ([Sonarworks](https://www.sonarworks.com/blog/learn/how-to-achieve-a-natural-sound-using-vocal-effects-plugins)). No controlled study measures how much any single step helps, so tune by ear.

## YouTube penalises templated content, not synthetic voices

The **YouTube Partner Program does not ban AI voices**. In July 2025 YouTube renamed its "repetitious content" policy to "inauthentic content" to target mass-produced videos, and using AI tools does not by itself disqualify a channel ([TechRepublic](https://techrepublic.com/article/news-youtube-ai-video-monetization-rules)). A July 2026 clarification named three problem categories: generic or repetitive content, unsatisfying or off-putting content, and AI personas giving advice on sensitive topics such as finance, law or medicine ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/20/youtube-clarifies-policies-around-ai-slop-and-upsetting-videos/)). Creator guides agree that **an AI voice narrating an original script is fine**. The formats at risk are slideshows without original commentary, TTS over stock footage with no analysis, and listicles where only the topic changes ([vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-ai-monetization/); [faceless.my](https://faceless.my/youtube/youtube-inauthentic-content-policy-faceless-ai-2026/)). Vendor blogs claim the July 2026 update also demonetised "synthetic narration over sourced, attributed information" ([deeporax](https://deeporax.com/blog/youtube-inauthentic-content-policy-2026)). No official page confirmed this, but it would affect documentaries directly, so read the live YPP policy page (support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392) before relying on any summary.

Disclosure is a separate rule from monetisation. Creators must label **realistic** altered or synthetic content, such as making a real person appear to say something or realistic footage of events that didn't happen ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491)). A made-up narrator voice like the channel's VoiceDesign creation probably doesn't trigger the label on its own. Cloning an identifiable real narrator would, and it would also break the consent clauses in almost every model licence. Viewers matter as much as policy here. In an Adobe Express survey, **77% of consumers said they trust human voices most and 48% named a lack of emotional authenticity as AI audio's biggest problem** ([StratCom Academy](https://stratcom.academy/2026/03/06/consumers-notice-ai-generated-voices-but-still-trust-humans-more/)). Another study found that trust falls once listeners are told a voice is AI ([ITBrief](https://itbrief.asia/story/uk-listeners-spot-ai-voices-faster-trust-them-less)). Each robotic tell you remove makes the narration more convincing to viewers.

## Conclusion

Free TTS has split into two groups. Open models that sound clearly better than Qwen3-TTS exist, but they need GPUs or ban commercial use. Hosted models that might sound better are free only under terms Google hasn't spelled out for commercial use. That leaves the channel's current Qwen setup as the best choice for this hardware and licence, and the bigger gains now come from workflow: a stronger noir-style reference clip, shorter chunks with varied pauses, and paragraph-level QA. Two tests are worth running before the next episode. First, render one script section with Gemini 3.8 Flash TTS using a designed detective voice. Second, clone the existing reference with Ming-3.0-tts 0.5B. Compare both blind against Qwen. Switch only if a challenger wins clearly and its terms check out.

All of this research came from search summaries, because Hugging Face, Artificial Analysis, ai.google.dev and YouTube Help could not be fetched directly. Before you rely on any licence or arena figure here, confirm it on the official page. Pay particular attention to the Gemini free-tier terms and Ming's LICENSE file.
