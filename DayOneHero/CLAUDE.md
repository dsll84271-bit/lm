# Day One Hero: project brief for Claude Code

Leo (England, 20) runs the YouTube channel **Day One Hero** (@dayonehero): animated superhero "what if" videos told with real physics. Every episode, the mascot **Dex** wakes up with a new power and finds out what it would really be like.

## Standing requirements from Leo
- Quality bar: "Don't give me anything that isn't a 9/10." Aim for Pixar/DreamWorks-level polish in everything: picture, voice, music, edit. Rate work honestly, and never call something 9/10 when it isn't.
- **Everything must be free** and allowed on a monetised channel. No paid tools, and nothing whose free tier bans commercial use. ElevenLabs was ruled out for this reason.
- Episodes run **just over 8:00**, so mid-roll ads unlock. Add real content to get there; don't pad.
- British English in narration and on screen.

## Dex (the mascot)
Chibi boy with a round face and short black anime hair. Yellow hoodie with the **hood always up** and cream drawstrings. Glossy blue anime eyes, pink blush, black trousers with a white side stripe, blue-and-white trainers. The look matches the Episode 1 thumbnail (`DayOneHero_Ep1_Thumbnail.jpg`).

## Hard rules
- **Never draw or animate existing characters** (Marvel, DC, My Hero Academia, anime, etc.), even as stickmen. Franchise power *names* may appear in "Hero Science" titles. The power is always given to Dex and drawn in Dex's own style: no costumes, no signature effects or colours, no move names.
- Physics numbers must be real and sourced. Rounded estimates are labelled as estimates.
- The YouTube "AI use" disclosure is ticked **No**, because the content is clearly animated and the narrator is not a cloned real voice.

## Pipeline (how the videos are made)
- **Visuals:** each segment is an HTML/SVG page with a JS `render(t)` function. `lib.js` holds the character rig (`chibi()`, `chibiSide()`), backgrounds and defs; `helpers.js` holds text, chips, wipes and gauges; `vid2/ep2.js` holds the Ep2 helpers. Frames are captured with Playwright (Chromium) at 30 fps and 1920×1080, then piped to ffmpeg (libx264, crf 18, veryfast, yuv420p).
  - Ep2 tooling: `python mk.py <seg>` builds `<seg>.html`, `python shot.py <seg> t1 t2…` writes test frames and a contact sheet to `shots/`, and `python render.py <seg>` writes `<seg>_silent.mp4`. Timings come from `t_<seg>.js`, generated by `timing.py` from narration lengths.
- **Voice:** Kokoro (kokoro-onnx) with voice **af_heart** ("Heart"), mastered with this ffmpeg chain:
  `highpass=f=75,lowshelf=g=2:f=160,equalizer=f=3200:t=q:w=1.2:g=3,highshelf=g=2:f=9000,acompressor=threshold=-20dB:ratio=3:attack=5:release=80:makeup=3,aecho=0.8:0.5:22:0.06,loudnorm=I=-16:TP=-1.5:LRA=7`, at 44.1 kHz.
  - Ep2 lines are in `vid2/tts/lines.py` and generated with `gen.py`.
- **Music and SFX:** synthesised with numpy (`vid/music.py`, `vid/music2.py`, `vid/sfx1.py`). The Ep2 versions are `vid2/audio.py` and `vid2/sfxmix.py`, which also mix voice, music and SFX with ducking.
- **Final:** segments are joined with the ffmpeg concat demuxer, then two-pass loudnorm takes the whole episode to **-14 LUFS, -1.5 dBTP**, with `+faststart`.
- Run Ep2 scripts from inside `vid2/`. They read `../vid/music.py` and `../vid/sfx1.py`.

## Honest weak spots and the upgrade plan (start here)
Next to the channels Leo benchmarks against (Awaken, IFANIME, ManifestingWhatIF), the writing holds up but the production is behind:
1. **Animation feels puppet-like.** Build "Rig 2.0": 25+ key poses with pose-to-pose arcs; anticipation, squash/stretch, overshoot; secondary motion (hood, drawstrings, breathing, natural blinks); richer expressions.
2. **Static camera.** Use shot variety (wide, medium, close-up; a cut every 2–4 s), push-ins, whip pans, tilted angles, real shake, depth of field, impact and smear frames, speed lines.
3. **Thin, repeated backgrounds.** Leo will generate empty background plates (no characters) in a flat cartoon style that matches Dex, using a free AI image tool. Add parallax layers, lighting and weather over them.
4. **Synthesised music sounds cheap.** Swap in tracks from the YouTube Audio Library that Leo downloads.
5. **Voice.** Try better free local TTS that this machine can now download (e.g. Chatterbox from Hugging Face). Check the licence allows commercial use before using any model.

**First task:** remake Ep2's car-crash beat (`vid2/sc_p.js`, the start of the segment up to the box landing) as a ~15-second **before/after** using Rig 2.0 and the new camera language. Show Leo both side by side before rebuilding anything else.

- Build steps:
  - `node check.js <seg>` checks every frame (errors or NaN).
  - `python shot.py <seg> t…` makes a contact sheet.
  - `python render.py <seg> 4` writes `<seg>_silent.mp4`.
  - `python mix_<seg>.py` writes `mix3_<seg>.wav`.
  - `python mux3.py <seg> <name>` writes `seg3_<seg>.mov` plus a −14 LUFS review copy in `../out/`.
  - `python final3.py` writes the master and the 720p share copy.
- `fixrange.py <seg> t0 t1` re-renders part of a segment and splices it in.
- The master uses two-pass loudnorm to −14 LUFS, then a 4×-oversampled limiter; true peak measures −1.9 dBTP.
